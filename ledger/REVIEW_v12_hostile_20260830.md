# Two hostile but fair reviews of DRAFT v1.2 (2026-08-30)

Reviewed: DRAFT_v12_jeffrey_20260830.md (title, abstract, intro, methods) and DRAFT_v12_sections_20260830.md (results, discussion, limitations, conclusion), read against ledger/related/RELATED_WORK_REVIEW_20260829.md and ledger/FINDINGS_expert_specialization_v2_20260829.html. Written by Claude, 2026-08-30. Every objection names the sentence it targets, gives a severity, points to what the ledger already holds that answers it, and gives the smallest text change that would defuse it. Nothing in the drafts was edited.

Severity scale. Blocking: a reviewer of this camp recommends reject until it is fixed. Major: must be addressed in a revision; unaddressed, it costs the paper the reviewer's vote. Minor: fix in the camera-ready.

## Reviewer 1. The "specialization is a myth" camp

### (a) Summary as this reviewer would state it

The authors capture router logits on a community uncensoring fine-tune of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B under greedy decoding, split prompt tokens from generated tokens, and pool routed weight over each block and over all 40 layers by expert index. On prompt tokens they reproduce the router-collapse result: one high-frequency expert leads for 18 of 20 subjects. On generated tokens the leading experts disperse, and the top-8 set computed over a generated segment about one subject picks out the same subject in an independently generated answer 23 of 24 and 36 of 38 times, against a shuffle null near 0.06. Prefill rows of the same questions identify at 0.15 to 0.17. Position, drift, and label-shuffle controls are run; a register change (biographies instead of explanations) drops identification to 0.54; a long homogeneous technical prompt shows no prefill-generation split at all; and biasing the four leading "philosophy" experts off leaves the philosophy answers unchanged. From this the paper concludes, in its title, that specialization is not a myth. My reading is that the paper has produced a clean and useful measurement of subject-conditioned routing recurrence on generated text and then attached it to a thesis its own evidence does not support: the geometry account of Wang, Hayou and Nalisnick predicts exactly this recurrence, and the paper says as much in its Discussion.

### (b) Objections

1. The title claims to rebut a thesis the paper's own Discussion endorses.
Targets: title "Specialization Is Not a Myth"; Discussion "The mechanism proposed by Wang, Hayou and Nalisnick, that routing is a linear readout of hidden-state geometry, is consistent with everything here and offers the simplest account of it"; Conclusion "Specialization is visible in the writing."
Severity: blocking.
The Myth paper's claim is that routing reflects hidden-state geometry rather than domain expertise. If generated chemistry tokens have chemistry-shaped hidden states and the router reads them, the top-8 set recurs by subject with no expert holding chemistry knowledge. That is the Myth thesis, not its refutation, and the paper's causal result (leaders removable, answers unchanged) is evidence for it. What the ledger answers: RELATED_WORK_REVIEW section 1 says the defensible gap is "narrower and more defensible than 'experts specialize'"; section 3 item 8 says to call the object a routing-side crew and not claim it measures what the expert writes; F7 in the findings says "removable, not load-bearing." Smallest change: retitle to name the measurement, for instance "Generation-Time Expert Sets Recur by Subject in a Mixture-of-Experts Model," and add one sentence to the Introduction after "We use this subject-conditioned recurrence as the operational signature of specialization": "This is routing specialization in the sense of Herbst et al. (which experts are selected), not functional specialization (what the selected experts compute), and it is compatible with the geometry account of Wang, Hayou and Nalisnick."

2. The conflict the paper claims to reconcile does not exist in the cited papers.
Targets: Introduction "Prior work presents two views of expert organization"; "These results place the apparently conflicting findings of prior work into two routing regimes"; Discussion "The two routing regimes reconcile the prior findings without rejecting any of them."
Severity: major.
None of the five papers claims that generation routing is not organized by subject; four never measure a generated token and the fifth measures four hand-picked pairs. The ledger says this in RELATED_WORK_REVIEW section 1 ("None of the five tests whether generation routing is organized by subject") and section 2 ("No paper contradicts findings 1 to 4 and 6 with data"). A reconciliation framing invites the reply that the authors set up a disagreement to resolve it. Smallest change: replace "apparently conflicting findings of prior work" with "the prior findings, all taken on input tokens," and rewrite the first Discussion sentence as "Every prior measurement we cite was taken on tokens supplied as input; our prefill numbers agree with each of them. The open question, left by Wang, Hayou and Nalisnick, was how the rollout is organized."

3. Recurrence of a routing set is not distinguished from recurrence of a token distribution.
Targets: Introduction "If the same expert set returns for the same subject after its wording, position, and surrounding context change, while different subjects recruit different sets, then the router is organizing computation by local subject"; Discussion "A subject's set may well be a coalition of fine-grained operation experts that co-occur when the model writes about chemistry."
Severity: major.
A chemistry answer contains formulas, element names, numerals and Latin morphology at rates no other subject shares. Under Herbst et al.'s fine-grained view the "chemistry set" is the union of the experts that handle those token types, and it recurs because the token mix recurs. The paper concedes the point and then keeps the word specialization. The design has no token-class control: nothing shows that subject-neutral tokens inside a chemistry segment (function words, punctuation, newlines) route to the chemistry set. What the ledger answers: F2 notes the split is present in the first 64 generated tokens, which weakly argues against slow accumulation but says nothing about token class. Smallest change: add to Limitations: "The design does not separate subject from the subject-typical distribution of token types; a token-class split (content versus function tokens within a segment) would, and is not available from the surviving layer-averaged tensors." And use "subject-conditioned routing" rather than "specialization" wherever the result is stated.

4. Register is a co-equal organizer, and the title says subject.
Targets: title "Subject-Specific Expert Sets"; abstract "larger register changes account for some identification failures"; Results register paragraph "Pairs involving B identify at 44 of 82 (0.54)."
Severity: major.
The paper's own numbers say a register change with the subject held fixed moves the generation set as far as a subject change does: HVAC technical versus experiential gives generation overlap 0.00 (F4, the full size of a domain change), changing the prompt type in the 50-pair run gives 0.13 (about what a domain change gives, 0.06), and identification falls from 0.96 to 0.54 when the task changes to biographies. "Some identification failures" is not an honest size for a drop of that magnitude. The 95 to 96% figure holds because A, C and P are all third-person expository. What the ledger answers: F4 in full, and F2a's reading that B "is F2 and F4 in a single measurement." Smallest change: in the abstract replace "larger register changes account for some identification failures" with "when the requested task and voice change, identification falls to 0.54; register moves the generation set about as much as subject does, and the 95 to 96% figure is for matched register." Consider "Subject- and Register-Specific" in the title if the title survives objection 1.

5. The causal result supports the reviewer's reading and is not reported as a method or a result.
Targets: Discussion "Biasing the four philosophy leaders off leaves 15 of 20 subject winners in place, per-subject overlap with baseline at 0.79, and the philosophy answers on topic and of the same length"; "the causal test supports that reading."
Severity: major.
The experiment is nowhere in Methods and nowhere in Results; its first appearance is a Discussion sentence with numbers. The ledger (F7) also records that the folder README describes the design wrongly, that a +2 bias already derails output, and that there is no dose-response interior. As it stands the result says the identified experts can be covered for by others, which is the Myth camp's position. Smallest change: either add a Methods paragraph (four experts, symmetric bias sweep at every layer, 9 runs times 60 prompts, what was measured) and a short Results paragraph stating "removable, not load-bearing," or cut the numbers from the Discussion and say only that a removal test is reported in the data repository.

6. The operative variable may be span length and content homogeneity, not prefill versus generation.
Targets: abstract "For short mixed prompts, prefill carries the wording of the question, while generation carries the subject of the answer"; Scope "On a long, coherent technical paragraph followed by a technical answer ... prefill and generation share the set: overlap 0.60 to 0.78."
Severity: major.
The Scope section shows that when the prompt is long and about one thing, prefill already routes to the subject set, and a narrow-topic prompt (medical triage) routes to its topic set in prefill. So the contrast the abstract describes as prefill versus generation is, on the paper's own evidence, a 20-token heterogeneous prompt versus a hundreds-of-tokens homogeneous answer. The findings file (F11) says exactly this: "the 'regime effect' is a content-and-register effect that short, mixed prompts expose." Smallest change: state it in the abstract in those words, and add to Limitations: "We did not replay generated answers as prompts; a teacher-forced prefill of the same text would show whether the generation set is a property of producing the text or of the text itself."

7. The Standing Committee comparison mixes incomparable statistics, and the GLM "reproduction" is looser than the sentence says.
Targets: Results "This is the pattern the Standing Committee analysis reports on Qwen3-30B-A3B (cross-domain Jaccard 0.87, minimum 0.53) ... their minimum sits inside our range"; "expert 10 wins 12 of 20 subjects (15 prompts each), with five distinct winners and Herfindahl 0.41."
Severity: minor.
0.87 is a mean of full-softmax expected weight at the last prompt token over nine pooled domains; 0.51 is mean routed weight over all prompt tokens over 20 subjects. Comparing one paper's minimum with another's mean proves nothing. On GLM the ledger (F10) says the top five defaults sit within 0.002 of one another and 8 of 20 winners change identity between analysis choices. Smallest change: replace the "minimum sits inside our range" clause with "overlap statistics are not comparable across papers (unit, token position, pooling differ); both find one dominant prefill set," and add to the GLM sentence "the top five defaults sit within 0.002 of one another, so winner identity is unstable there."

8. The eight "prompt types" of the 50-pair run are tasks and registers, not subjects.
Targets: Results "Across the 50-pair run, a subject's generation set on the official checkpoint identifies the same prompt type on the HauhauCS checkpoint in 7 of 8 types."
Severity: minor.
Fact check, legal, medical, and "what is it like to be you" are not a subject axis. That these sets recur across checkpoints is evidence that task-conditioned or register-conditioned sets recur, which is consistent with objection 4 and not with a subject claim. Smallest change: "a prompt type's generation set" and delete "subject" from that sentence.

9. The reasoning-preamble reply needs the capture setting stated.
Targets: Discussion "Their attribution of prefill collapse to reasoning preambles does not carry over: the flat default appears on a non-reasoning fine-tune and on the official checkpoint with no chain of thought."
Severity: minor, becoming major if thinking tokens are in any generation block.
Qwen3.5-35B-A3B is a hybrid-thinking model. The Methods never say whether thinking was disabled, how (template flag, empty think block), or whether the generation blocks were checked for think tokens. Smallest change: one Methods sentence stating the template and thinking setting used for every capture and that no think tokens appear in any generation block, and cite the ERNIE-PT null from the Myth paper as the contrast the ledger (RELATED_WORK section 7) recommends.

### (c) Two questions the drafts do not answer

1. If you feed a generated chemistry answer back to the model as a prompt (teacher-forced prefill of the model's own text), does its prefill top-8 set match the generation set that produced it? If yes, "generation" is not the operative variable and the paper is about span length and content homogeneity.

2. Within one generated chemistry segment, do the subject-neutral tokens (function words, punctuation, line breaks) route to the chemistry set, or is the set carried by the content tokens alone? Herbst et al.'s view predicts the latter; a subject-organized router predicts the former.

## Reviewer 2. Methods and statistics

### (a) Summary as this reviewer would state it

The paper's primary evidence is one greedy generation per packed prompt on one Q8_0 community fine-tune, three packed prompts in total, plus a 60-prompt probe (20 subjects, 3 answers each) whose per-prompt tensors did not survive. Expert sets are top-8 by routed weight, layer-pooled by expert index over 40 layers. Identification is argmax Jaccard across design pairs, with denominators of 24 and 38 for the headline cells and 168 overall, reported with Wilson intervals. A prefill control repeats the procedure on question spans that were rescaled across a 392-to-446 tokenizer mismatch. A second checkpoint contributes a 12-prompt, 5-subject, 256-token design (11 of 12 nearest-neighbour pairing) and a 100-prompt run over 8 prompt types. A 122B checkpoint contributes a prefill replication and a generation block with about 45% post-answer spill; GLM contributes prefill only. The measurements are recomputed and reproduce to the digit, and the authors disclose the archive defects. The problems are in the inferential layer: the headline cells are the best two of twelve ordered pairs, the queries are not independent, the prefill comparator differs from the generation comparator in span length, composition and alignment, the "prespecified" claim is not supported by the ledger, and layer pooling by expert index is a known-lossy operation on the paper's own evidence.

### (b) Objections

1. The headline cells are the best two of twelve, and the one design pair that most cleanly isolates "same subject, same register, different order" identifies at 0.58 for generation and 0.68 for prefill.
Targets: abstract "generation-based expert sets identify the subject at 95 to 96% in Qwen3.5-35B"; Introduction "recur for the same subject across positions, prompt design"; Results "The one prefill pair that identifies well is A to C, 27 of 40 (0.68), where the two packed prompts share their wording verbatim."
Severity: major.
From the per-pair table in the sections draft: A to C generation 7 of 12, C to A 7 of 12 (0.58), while A to C prefill is 14 of 20 and C to A 13 of 20 (0.68). So for the one pair where the same twelve subjects sit in both directions on both sides, prefill identifies better than generation. The 95 to 96% cells are the two whose reference is the three-answer probe profile, a smoother object than any single packed segment. The paper does not confront this. What the ledger answers: the per-pair table (sections A.2) and the position control (matched-subject 0.194 versus 0.020) show a real but noisy signal between packed designs. Smallest change: put the per-pair table in the main text, add one sentence: "Between the two packed expository prompts, identification is 14 of 24 (0.58); the strong result is identification against the independent probe, whose three-answer profile is a lower-variance reference," and carry 117 of 168 (0.70) into the abstract next to 95 to 96%.

2. Bidirectional queries are not independent, so the Wilson intervals overstate precision.
Targets: Methods "Thus A to P contributes 12 + 12 = 24 queries and C to P contributes 19 + 19 = 38"; Results "23 of 24 queries (0.96; Wilson 95% CI 0.80 to 0.99)."
Severity: major.
If segment A_i's nearest candidate in P is P_i, P_i's nearest in A is very likely A_i; the two directions reuse the same 12 and 20 sets. One-way the counts are 11 of 12 and 12 of 12 (A, P), 18 of 19 and 18 of 19 (C, P). The 168 pool reuses each subject up to twelve times. Smallest change: report one-way counts as the primary numbers, compute the Wilson interval on the smaller unique count (11 of 12; 18 of 19), and add to Statistical analysis: "Queries in the two directions of a pair share sets and are not independent; intervals are descriptive."

3. The controls are described as prespecified; the ledger says the identification test and its nulls were designed after the audit.
Targets: Methods "No multiple-comparison correction is applied because the wording, position, drift, register, and shuffle controls were prespecified and are all reported."
Severity: major (integrity, and a reviewer can check the ledger dates).
FROZEN_PREDICTIONS_20260828T2115 covers P1 to P8, which concern the default expert, expert 114, dialect and layers. F2a is marked "added in v2" on 2026-08-29 and CREW_REPRODUCIBILITY is dated 08-28. Nothing about cross-design identification or its four nulls was frozen before the data were seen. Smallest change: "No multiple-comparison correction is applied; the identification test and its four controls were fixed before they were run on the packed tensors, none was preregistered, and every cell is reported."

4. The prefill comparator differs from the generation comparator in span length, span alignment, row composition and wording relation, and the paper does not say which of these carries the 0.96 versus 0.17 gap.
Targets: Methods "so question spans were rescaled proportionally and may be off by one or two positions; generation spans are exact"; Introduction "comparing each generated segment with the same question asked independently"; abstract "compared with 15 to 17% for prefill."
Severity: blocking for the prefill contrast, which is half the abstract.
Four problems stack. (i) Length: prefill spans are about 20 tokens, generation segments are roughly 100 to 2,000; a top-8 set from 20 tokens is far noisier than one from hundreds, so identification will fall for that reason alone. (ii) Alignment: the tokenizer mismatch is 54 tokens on a 446-token prompt (14%). If the difference is a template block at the start, a proportional rescale misplaces a question at HF offset 300 by about 13 tokens, more than half a 20-token span; "off by one or two positions" is asserted, not measured. (iii) Composition: what the probe P's prefill rows contain (template tokens, all three questions pooled, position-resolved or not) is never stated. If P's prefill profile pools template tokens, it is default-dominated by construction and the A to P prefill failure is manufactured. (iv) Wording: A to C prefill identifies at 0.68 because the wording is verbatim; the paper says P asks "the same questions," yet A to P prefill is 0.17. Either the wording differs (then "same questions" is wrong) or packed context moves the prefill set of identical text (then "prefill follows wording" is wrong). What the ledger answers: F2a records the rescale caveat and that generation spans are exact; F2 records the split present in the first 64 generated tokens, which is the seed of a length-matched test. Smallest change that defuses all four: rebuild the prefill spans from the llama.cpp prompt token IDs in the capture metadata (or identify the 54-token block and use an offset), report a plus or minus 5 token shift sensitivity, state P's prefill row composition and the exact wording relation between P and A/C, and add a length-matched row: identification of each packed segment against P using its first 32 and 64 generated tokens only.

5. Layer pooling by expert index is known-lossy on the paper's own evidence, and the sentence offered in its defence is a non sequitur.
Targets: Methods "Layers were pooled by an unweighted mean over the per-layer W vectors, indexed by expert number"; sections A.1 "The layer-pooled profile therefore summarizes a signal that is flat across depth rather than concentrated in a few layers."
Severity: major.
F6 in the findings: on GLM, pooling across layers erases domain clustering entirely (every pair at cosine 0.99) while the (layer, expert) level clusters semantically; on 122B the DeltaNet and softmax layers use disjoint expert vocabularies, so a pooled table blends two routing systems; and the 35B's "14 distinct winners per layer" is a dispersion count, not a recurrence measure, so it does not show that identification holds per layer. The independent probe survives per layer (20 by 40 by 256) but has no second design; the packed tensors are layer-averaged. What the ledger answers: the 12-prompt official design and the 50-pair run retain full raw tensors at all 40 layers. Smallest change: compute per-layer nearest-neighbour subject pairing on the 12-prompt official design (and per-layer prompt-type pairing on the 50-pair run) and report the fraction of layers at which pairing beats chance; replace the "therefore summarizes" sentence with "per-layer identification is available only on the official-checkpoint designs, where it holds at N of 40 layers."

6. The drift control is undefined for probe targets and its chance value differs between the two drafts.
Targets: Introduction "still selects the correct subject 83% of the time, against 33% by chance"; Methods "Adjacent-segment null: candidates are restricted to the previous, same, and next subject in the target's order."
Severity: major for the definition, minor for the number.
The probe P has no order in which subjects were generated; "previous and next" in P is a list position, not a temporal neighbour, so the drift test is only meaningful when the candidate design is A, B or C. The 139 of 168 count includes P-target queries. The sections draft corrects chance to 0.354; the intro still says 33%. Smallest change: restrict the drift null to packed-target queries, report that count and its exact chance, and change the intro to match.

7. Chance for the 12-prompt official design is 0.15, not 1 in 11.
Targets: Results "generation sets pair prompts by subject in 11 of 12 nearest-neighbour queries (0.92; CI 0.65 to 0.99; chance 1 in 11)."
Severity: minor, but an arithmetic error a reviewer will find.
Finance has four cells, so a finance query has 3 same-subject candidates of 11; the other eight prompts have 1 of 11. Mean chance is (4 times 3/11 plus 8 times 1/11) over 12, which is 0.15. Mutual nearest neighbours are also not independent, so the Wilson interval on 12 is optimistic. Smallest change: "chance 0.15 (finance queries have three same-subject candidates)."

8. An exploratory number computed on 45% spill sits in Results, and the 122B layer-mixing problem is undisclosed.
Targets: Results "The 122B generation block, exploratory because its trimmer failed, shows 18 distinct winners"; Limitations "The 122B contributes a clean prefill replication and an exploratory generation result whose block is about 45% post-answer spill."
Severity: major.
The findings file (section 4) says the 18-distinct result "needs a proper re-trim, which needs tensors we no longer have," and that E48, the leading untrimmed expert, is a spill expert. A number the authors say cannot be trusted should not appear in Results. F6 adds that on 122B the DeltaNet and softmax layers have zero top-8 overlap, so the 48-layer pooled prefill table (13 of 20) blends two routing systems; the drafts never say so. Smallest change: move the 122B generation sentence to an appendix with the spill fraction, and add to the 122B prefill sentence "pooled over DeltaNet and softmax layers, whose expert vocabularies are disjoint on this checkpoint."

9. "Five checkpoints" in the abstract reads as five generation replications; it is two checkpoints of one architecture.
Targets: abstract "We analyze five checkpoints across the Qwen3.5 and GLM-4.7-Flash families"; Conclusion "we reproduce that result on five checkpoints across two families and two gating mechanisms."
Severity: major (framing).
The generation evidence is HauhauCS 35B (primary) and official 35B (12 prompts, 5 subjects, 256 tokens; and 8 prompt types). GLM has no generation capture; 122B's is unusable. The Limitations say this plainly; the abstract does not. Smallest change: after the "five checkpoints" sentence add "The generation-time result is established on two checkpoints of one architecture; the other three contribute prefill only."

10. The Conclusion attributes the 95 to 96% figure to the official checkpoint.
Targets: Conclusion "return for the same subject under a different prompt design at 95 to 96% identification against 6% by chance, on both the official and the fine-tuned checkpoint."
Severity: minor.
The official checkpoint's number is 11 of 12 in a different design with 256-token answers. Smallest change: "at 95 to 96% on the fine-tuned checkpoint and 11 of 12 on the official checkpoint."

11. Provenance sentences contradict the Methods paragraph they follow.
Targets: Provenance "the official-checkpoint domain probe used llama.cpp build 8493 (1772701f)"; "the GLM prefill captures use commit 6658925."
Severity: minor, but it is the kind of inconsistency that makes a reviewer distrust every other number.
The sections draft (section B) records that build 8493 is the HauhauCS 60-prompt probe and the official checkpoint never ran it, and that commit 6658925 is the GLM register generation runs under llama.cpp Q8, while the GLM domain prefill came from BF16 weights under a Transformers hook, as the first Methods paragraph says. Smallest change: apply both corrections from sections B, and list the llama.cpp build per design (A, B, C, P, official 12-prompt, 50-pair), since greedy decoding is not token-identical across builds.

12. The paper names experts that its own Limitations say cannot be named, and the segmentation is model-chosen with unreported lengths.
Targets: Results "archaeology recruits experts 191, 80 and 135 in A, in C and in the independent probe; neuroscience 54 and 24 in A, B and P; ..."; Limitations "Only four generation winners on the 35B lead by a margin that survives dropping one prompt"; Methods "Answer spans were assigned to subjects at the model's own markdown headers, giving 12 segments in A, 15 in B, and 19 in C."
Severity: minor.
Recurrence across three designs is a fair reason to name an expert, but the Limitations say only four names are safe at n = 3 in P; the Results should say these are illustrative. Segment token counts are never given, so a reader cannot judge whether the two misses in C to P or the B failures are the shortest segments. Set-size sensitivity was run for the prefill-generation overlap but not for identification. Smallest change: label the named experts "illustrative," add a segment-length column to the per-pair table (or an appendix table of segment lengths), and report identification at top-15 alongside top-8.

13. Quantization and padding are unstated confounds.
Targets: Methods "the official instruction-tuned checkpoint (Q8_0, ggml-org) and the HauhauCS uncensored fine-tune derived from it (Q8_0)"; "each padded to 446 prompt tokens."
Severity: minor.
All Qwen router logits come from Q8_0 GGUF weights; GLM from BF16. The paper treats the quantized router as the model's router without a sentence saying so. "Padded to 446 tokens" is not explained: padded with what, and were the padding tokens included in the prefill profile? Smallest change: one sentence on quantization, and replace "padded to" with what was actually done (or state that padding tokens were excluded from every prefill row).

### (c) Two questions the drafts do not answer

1. What are the token lengths of the 46 packed segments, and how does identification accuracy vary with segment length? In particular, what is identification against P when each segment is truncated to its first 32 and 64 generated tokens, which is the only way to compare against 20-token prefill spans on equal footing?

2. Are the probe's 60 questions verbatim identical to the packed questions, what tokens make up each probe prompt's prefill rows (template included or excluded, three questions pooled or one), and was thinking disabled and by what template setting? Without these three facts the 0.17 prefill number cannot be interpreted.

## The three changes that would most raise acceptance odds, in order

1. Reframe the claim to match the evidence, with no new data. Drop "Specialization Is Not a Myth" from the title; define the object in the Introduction as routing specialization in Herbst's sense and state that it is compatible with the geometry account; state in the abstract that the generation result is on two checkpoints of one architecture, that identification is 0.70 over all design pairs and 0.96 against the independent probe, that a register change drops it to 0.54, and that the operative condition is a short heterogeneous prompt followed by a long homogeneous answer. Replace the "reconcile conflicting findings" framing with "prior work measured input tokens; the rollout's organization was open." This removes Reviewer 1's blocking objection and Reviewer 2's objections 1, 4 (in part), 9 and 10 at the cost of one afternoon of rewriting.

2. Repair the prefill comparator, because the abstract's central contrast rests on it. Rebuild the packed prefill spans from llama.cpp token IDs rather than a proportional rescale and report a shift sensitivity; state the probe's prefill row composition and the exact wording relation between probe and packed questions; add a length-matched identification row (first 32 and 64 generated tokens against P). If a box-hour is available, the same run that closes this also closes the biggest remaining holes: rerun the packed and probe designs on the official checkpoint with thinking explicitly disabled, keeping per-token per-layer router tensors and the prompt token IDs, with ten prompts per subject, and add a teacher-forced prefill of the generated answers. That single capture answers Reviewer 1's question 1, Reviewer 2's objections 4, 5 and 12, the n = 3 winner fragility, and the "why a community fine-tune" question at once.

3. Statistical and reporting hygiene, all from the ledger. Replace "prespecified" with the true history; report one-way identification counts and label intervals descriptive; restrict the drift null to packed targets and harmonize 0.354 across drafts; correct the 12-prompt chance to 0.15; move the 122B generation number to an appendix and disclose the DeltaNet/softmax vocabulary split; put the causal bias experiment into Methods and Results with the "removable, not load-bearing" reading or remove its numbers from the Discussion; fix the two provenance sentences; compute per-layer nearest-neighbour pairing on the official 12-prompt design where all-layer tensors survive. None of these needs a GPU.
