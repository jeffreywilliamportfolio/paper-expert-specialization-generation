# Related-work read: arXiv 2604.17837

Source read: arXiv HTML (https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17837, v1) plus the PDF header. All quotes verbatim unless marked (paraphrase).

## 1. Citation

Charles Ye (Independent), Bo Yuan (Georgia Institute of Technology), Lee Sharkey (Goodfire). "Polysemantic Experts, Monosemantic Paths: Routing as Control in MoEs." arXiv:2604.17837v1 [cs.AI], 20 Apr 2026. PDF header: "To appear at the ICLR 2026 Workshop on Representational Alignment (Re-Align)".

## 2. Models, sizes, experts, top-k, data

Six models (Appendix C, Table 1): Qwen3-30B-A3B (3B/30B, 48 MoE layers, 128 experts, k=8); GPT-OSS-20B (4B/20B, 24 layers, 32 experts, k=4); GLM-4.5-Air (12B/106B, 45 layers, 128 experts, k=8, shared experts); OLMoE (1B/7B, 16 layers, 64 experts, k=8); Granite-4-Tiny (1B/7B, 40 layers, 62 experts, k=6); DeepSeek-v2-Lite (2B/16B, 26 layers, 64 experts, k=6, shared experts). No Qwen3.5, no GLM-4.7-Flash.

Data: "All results from Section 3 use a 2 million token dataset sampled at sequence level from a mix of the C4 (Raffel et al., 2023) and HPLTv2 (Burchell et al., 2025) datasets. For each model, we run forward passes, collect representations h_l and expert selections at each layer l. For path analysis results, we use a 10m token dataset sampled from the same sources." Path analysis: "We collect paths over ~10M multilingual tokens (EN/ZH/ES) from C4 and HPLT, using gpt-oss-20b across mid-layers l=8,...,16". Figure 1 colon data: "we iteratively prompt several auxiliary LLMs to produce text containing the token ':' in each of three syntactic roles with high diversity, then randomly sample across auxiliary generators" (Appendix G). Note that even this LLM-generated text is then run through the studied model as input, not generated by it.

## 3. Definitions of "specialization" and "expert usage"; pooled or per-token; layers

They never define "expert usage" or give a specialization score. Their unit is the per-token path: "for a contiguous band of L layers, each token's path is the tuple of L top-1 expert IDs it traverses. We group tokens sharing identical paths and examine the resulting clusters." Footnote 3: "We probe the top-1 expert throughout." So the analysis is strictly per token, top-1 only (not top-8, not routed weights), and "specialization" is used loosely: "Because each routing decision is low-bandwidth, this rotation forces compositional specialization across layers." Layers: all MoE layers for the control/content probes; paths on gpt-oss-20b layers 8 to 16 ("the depth range where surface features are most absent from the control signal"); Figure 1 paths across all 48 layers of Qwen3-30B-A3B.

Answer to the question in our prompt: "paths" are measured across layers on single tokens. One token, one tuple of top-1 expert IDs across a band of layers. There is no pooling over a token set; the cluster is the set of tokens that happen to share an identical tuple. This is the opposite axis from our crew (top-8 by mean routed weight over a token set, one layer at a time).

## 4. Prefill vs generation (the decisive item)

They do not separate the two, and they measure only prefill. Every measurement is a forward pass on pre-existing text: "For each model, we run forward passes, collect representations h_l and expert selections at each layer l." The path data is "~10M multilingual tokens (EN/ZH/ES) from C4 and HPLT". The colon dataset is produced by "auxiliary LLMs", then fed to the studied model. There is no sentence anywhere in the paper about sampled or generated tokens, decoding, temperature, or a generation block. The word "generate" appears only for the auxiliary-LLM colon corpus ("Sequences are generated for balanced category representation"). So: prefill only, on web corpus text, teacher-forced by construction. Their claims about what routing "encodes" are claims about routing over input text.

## 5. Subject / domain during generation

Not tested during generation (there is no generation). Domain is not a variable in their design at all. They probe three surface features, "language, token ID, and sequence position", and find them in the content channel: "Across all six models and every layer, these features reside primarily in h_l^blind, not h_l^vis (Appendix F)." The semantic groupings in Figure 4 (Exhibitions, Financial Performance, Overseas, Nutritional Substances, Transport Hubs, Brand Names) are post-hoc labels on path clusters, not domains they conditioned on. The one quantitative number for semantic organization is lexical diversity: "h_l^vis clusters, by contrast, group diverse tokens by shared function (4.3 unique IDs)" versus "1.1 unique token IDs per cluster" for content and "1.4 unique IDs" for the full hidden state, on "100 cross-layer clusters from each subspace" with "10 token samples per cluster". Their own framing of domain: "The intuitive model of a 'committee of specialists' where each expert masters a distinct domain has been largely invalidated (Jiang et al., 2024; Xue et al., 2024)."

## 6. Headline claim and evidence

"While individual experts remain polysemantic, expert paths become monosemantic, clustering tokens by semantic function across languages and surface forms."

Evidence:
- Figure 1: 500 top-1 paths of ":" in three roles on Qwen3-30B-A3B form visibly distinct bundles (qualitative, shared Sugiyama layout).
- Figure 4: tokens sharing an identical 9-layer top-1 path in gpt-oss-20b form cross-lingual semantic groups (qualitative examples).
- Control-subspace clusters: 4.3 unique token IDs per cluster vs 1.1 (content) and 1.4 (full h_l), with "≥95% pass screening" for interpretability.
- Probes: h_l^vis predicts current-layer top-1 expert "~99%" but next layer "~35%"; h_l^blind predicts next layer "~65-75%".
- Top 2% highest-magnitude dimensions predict top-1 expert "38-78%" (main text; Table 3 caption says "30-80%") vs "1-4%" random.

## 7. Causal tests

None in our sense. No ablation, no router bias, no expert removal, no steering. "Causal" in the paper means the algebraic guarantee of the decomposition: "By construction, R_l^T h_l^blind = 0: the router-blind component cannot influence expert selection. This is not a statistical claim, but a mathematical guarantee." Their "Causal validation" (Appendix D) is a probe: "a probe seeing only the top 2% of dimensions can predict the router's choice with 30-80% accuracy". Intervention is left as future work: "Perturbing control could steer expert selection without corrupting content ... Whether this orthogonality survives into downstream behavior is an open question".

## 8. Terms

- "polysemantic" / "monosemantic": "Individual experts are polysemantic; expert paths are not." Avoid adopting "polysemantic expert" for our leaders; our claim is about crews on token sets, not concept purity of one expert.
- "expert paths" / "trajectory": "The natural unit of interpretability in MoEs is not the expert—it is the trajectory." Reuse the word "path" only with their per-token meaning; our crew is not a path.
- "control signal" / "content channel", "router-visible" / "router-blind": the paper's own vocabulary; cite, do not reuse as ours.
- "committee of specialists": "The intuitive model of a 'committee of specialists' where each expert masters a distinct domain has been largely invalidated". This is the phrase a reviewer will throw at us; note it.
- "Routers amplify, they don't integrate." and "Control rotates; content accumulates." are their slogans.
- "surface features": "language, token identity, and position". Useful: they put dialect-like surface variation in the router-blind channel.
- No use of "generalist", "standing committee", "router collapse", "default expert", "prefill", or "generation".

## 9. Agreement with our findings 1-6

1. Prefill default expert vs generation distinct winners: SILENT on generation; partially AGREES on prefill being coarse: "Individual routing decisions are coarse—each one collapses many tokens onto the same expert, discarding the detail that makes h_l rich."
2. Crew turnover at subject boundaries, crew identifies subject 95-96%: SILENT (no generation, no subject variable, no token-set crews). Closest: "Paths cluster by meaning, not surface form."
3. Register moves crews, dialect a no-op: AGREES in spirit on dialect: "Language, token identity, and position are carried in content but do not drive expert selection through the core of the network". SILENT on register.
4. Long coherent prompt shares crew across prefill and generation: SILENT.
5. Leaders removable, not load-bearing: SILENT (no intervention). Weakly compatible: "no single routing decision can express fine-grained specialization."
6. Fine-tunes never touched routers: SILENT.

## 10. What they did that we did not, and the reverse

They: six architectures (including GLM-4.5-Air and gpt-oss); an exact orthogonal decomposition of the residual stream by SVD of the router matrix; probes for current vs next-layer expert from each channel; cross-layer cosine of the two channels; MI probes for language, token ID, position; per-token multi-layer path clustering on 10M tokens; multilingual (EN/ZH/ES) data.

We: generation-block routing at all (they have none); prefill vs generation kept separate; top-8 crews with routed weights rather than top-1; token-set level crews per subject and register; cross-design transfer test with chance and shuffle nulls; a scoping condition (long coherent prompt); expert biasing as a causal test; fine-tune vs base router comparison; Qwen3.5-35B/122B and GLM-4.7-Flash.

## 11. Reviewer ammunition and honest reply

- "Ye et al. show the committee-of-specialists picture is invalidated; per-expert domain reads are the wrong unit." Reply: they say this about single experts on prefill over web text with top-1 routing. Our unit is a top-8 crew over a token set in the generation block; their prefill-only design cannot see the generation block, and their own line that a single routing decision "collapses many tokens onto the same expert" is consistent with our flat prefill default expert.
- "Their per-token path clustering already shows semantic organization without any generation; your generation effect may just be the same thing measured differently." Reply: possible, and the two measurements are on orthogonal axes (across layers on one token vs across tokens at one layer). The test that separates them is ours: identical questions give 15-17% subject identification on prefill rows and 95-96% on generation rows. A path-level version on generated tokens would be the right follow-up, not a rebuttal.
- "They find language and token identity in the router-blind channel, so routing is not about surface form; your register result might be surface form." Reply: their dialect-adjacent finding (language is router-blind) matches our dialect null; register is not a surface feature in their sense and they never test it.
- "Top-1 vs top-8." Reply: we should state that their paths are top-1 and ours are weighted top-8, and that the mean-weight crew is less sensitive to a single flipped argmax.

## 12. Related-work sentences

Ye, Yuan, and Sharkey (2026) argue that the single expert is the wrong unit for interpreting MoE routing and propose the per-token expert path, the tuple of top-1 expert IDs a token visits across a band of layers, as the unit that becomes monosemantic. They show on six MoE families that tokens sharing a mid-layer path group by function across languages, and that language, token identity, and position live in the part of the residual stream the router cannot read. Their measurements are forward passes over C4 and HPLT text, so every routing decision they analyze is a prefill decision on pre-existing tokens, and domain is not a variable in their design. Our work asks the complementary question at the token-set level and finds that the same routers that look flat on prompt tokens organize by subject and register once the model is producing the tokens itself.
